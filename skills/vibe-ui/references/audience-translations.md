# Audience and task translation

Don't predict aesthetics from age alone. Base direction on task frequency, risk, user literacy in the domain, product promises, environment of use, existing brand, and the amount of proof required.

## The six dials

1. **Trust burden:** casual exploration ↔ serious purchase, health, finance, critical operations.
2. **Proof burden:** an immediately demonstrable result ↔ complex benefits needing process/data/references.
3. **Emotional expression:** subtle ↔ bold; can be high even for serious products if aligned with the brand.
4. **Interaction familiarity:** novel gesture ↔ predictable industry-standard controls.
5. **Information density:** single action ↔ evaluation / comparison / high-frequency workflow.
6. **Urgency:** browse/discover ↔ book/request/buy/solve now.

Make choices from these dials rather than relying on demographic shortcuts.

## Consumer onboarding

- One meaningful first-use outcome, fast activation, short copy, intuitive sign-in.
- Illustration and rounded heavy type may be excellent if identity calls for it.
- Focal brand moment + clear next action. Ensure sign-in copy explains data handling and value when ambiguous.
- Avoid a long tutorial for the sake of using more screens.

## Local service business

- Immediate answers: what service, for whom, where, when, why trust, how to contact.
- Strong typographic hierarchy and a direct primary action, often call/request quote/book.
- Real work photography and service-specific motif can replace mascots and gradients.
- Mobile first-screen density generally higher than a single-action onboarding because trust, service area, and contact must coexist.
- Language should match customer terminology, not invented product categories.

## Professional / high-trust business

- Demonstrate competence, scope, process, people, and verified credentials without credential theater.
- Editorial or confident typography may be distinctive while interface patterns remain conservative.
- Distinctiveness through authentic imagery, composition, a domain-specific visual language, and precise copy.
- Clarity and legal correctness outrank entertainment.

## B2B SaaS

- Literal product description plus actual task/result (what input becomes what output); proof/integrations when critical.
- Show real interface slices or faithful product behavior, not vague decorative dashboards.
- Let a distinct brand voice live alongside serious UI controls.
- Allow more than one CTA when it genuinely serves different evaluation stages; preserve hierarchy.

## Internal workflows and dense dashboards

- Task efficiency, information chunking, discoverable controls, consistent status language, predictable navigation.
- Brand signature is quieter; use type, color roles, and interactions to organize rather than compete with data.
- Density is not a defect when the work requires it. Optimize scannability and reversibility.

## Premium creative / editorial

- Larger role for composition, photography, typographic choice, and rhythm.
- Restrained but bold is often stronger than luxury clichés or decorative excess.
- Keep actionable pathways legible even when art direction is unconventional.

## What "similar quality" means across contexts

The *surface* will change; the standards will not: recognizable character, disciplined hierarchy, selective emphasis, meaningful whitespace, coherent typography, grounded proof, expected controls, and polished responsiveness.
