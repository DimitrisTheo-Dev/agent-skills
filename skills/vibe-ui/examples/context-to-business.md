# Example: Commercial HVAC services website, no reference required

## Context

Audience: facilities managers and business owners needing reliable HVAC maintenance. Job: determine whether the provider serves their geography, works on commercial equipment, is credible, and can be contacted quickly. Trust burden: high because downtime is expensive. The site's real coverage, certifications, response commitments, and case evidence must be verified, not invented.

## Art direction

North star: **calm, capable, practical, precise**. Use a restrained palette based on actual branding, legible professional typography, clear service architecture, and authentic project or technician photography (if available). Build distinction around real domain specificity, not decorative stock illustrations. Navigation and contact controls should be familiar and accessible.

## First screen

A literal headline that states commercial HVAC service and coverage area; a short, verifiable supporting statement; primary CTA such as Request service; secondary contact only if useful; evidence above the fold if real. Avoid invented response-time guarantees and vague claims like 'redefining comfort'.

## Rhythm and components

Compact, readable information clusters; clear service categories; concise proof; generous but not wasteful vertical spacing; consistent button/radius/type system. On mobile, keep a straightforward contact path without obscuring content.

## QA

Test five-second comprehension, content authenticity, visual hierarchy, readable text and contrast, focus/touch interaction, real mobile layouts, and actual submission behavior. If evidence or visuals are missing, preserve placeholders as explicitly missing content rather than manufacturing proof.
